# How spoken language ID users handle low-confidence and accented windows

Researched 2026-10-30. The question: projects that run SpeechBrain's VoxLingua107 ECAPA-TDNN (`speechbrain/lang-id-voxlingua107-ecapa`) or a similar spoken language identifier (Whisper's language token, sherpa-onnx, NeMo AmberNet, Silero) all have to deal with windows the model is unsure about. How do they do it in their code? This note covers thresholds on the top language or on the candidates' combined probability, restricting the output to a candidate subset, margins between the top two languages, how windows are aggregated, the minimum amount of speech, how non-speech is rejected, and what is known about accented (L2) speech. It ends with what this means for Carbon's `LanguageVote`.

This builds on two earlier notes and does not redo them:

- [offline-spoken-language-identification.md](offline-spoken-language-identification.md) chose the model and measured it on TTS audio.
- [competitor-meeting-language-handling.md](competitor-meeting-language-handling.md) covers how meeting apps choose a language, and Whisper engine behaviour (§9).

Method: source files read via the GitHub API at the pinned commits below, plus GitHub issues, PRs and discussions, Hugging Face model cards and discussions, and the papers. Nothing was built or run. Anything I concluded myself is marked **(inference)**. Anything I could not settle is marked **(unverified)**.

Patterns, in one line each:

1. **Restricting to the person's languages and renormalizing is the norm** among apps with a fixed candidate list (Handy, Gladia, OVOS, transcribe.cpp). Whisper users ask for it too ([openai/whisper#849](https://github.com/openai/whisper/discussions/849), about French and English).
2. **Nobody else gates on the candidates' total mass.** Handy and transcribe.cpp compute it (`allowed_mass`) and only log it. Gladia and OVOS throw it away. Carbon's 0.1 floor has no precedent in the projects found here.
3. **Thresholds sit on the top language, not on the candidate set.** faster-whisper uses 0.5 over all languages. Kesha uses 0.5 because VoxLingua107 gives silence `nn` at 0.27. Vexa uses 0.3 and Attendee 0.5 (previous note). Gladia uses 0.95 on the renormalized share, but only to *switch away from* a confirmed language.
4. **Margins are mostly for logging.** Handy logs a pick under 1.5× the runner-up as "uncertain" and uses it anyway. Only Gladia's 0.95 acts as a real margin (about 19:1), close to Carbon's ln 20 **(inference)**.
5. **Aggregation is simple.** It is a majority vote (OLMoASR, faster-whisper's fallback, NeMo `get_label`, the accent paper), a duration-weighted mean of probabilities (speech-swift), or agreement between models (Curator, hayamimi, ESPnet, Kesha). Carbon's sum of clipped log-probabilities is stricter than all of them.
6. **Non-speech is rejected by VAD.** The few probability floors on the top language exist because a closed-set model gives silence a confident-looking label (Kesha's `nn`).
7. **Accent errors are a known, documented, unfixed property of the off-the-shelf model.** The fixes people use are: (a) a small classifier trained on ECAPA embeddings of in-domain audio, (b) longer context, (c) a second, lexical signal (text LID or a second model), (d) phoneme-sequence features.

## Findings by topic

### Restricting to candidates and renormalizing

- **Gladia** keeps only the allowed indices of the log-probabilities and applies a softmax over them. It returns the winner's renormalized share as `confidence`, plus the whole `constrained_distribution` ([voxlingua.py#L99-L135](https://github.com/gladiaio/realtime-multilingual-asr-router/blob/4e25d6489624db46dca627a59c35ad0222c2636b/src/realtime_multilingual_asr_router/lid/voxlingua.py#L99-L135)). The share of the full distribution that fell on the candidates is never computed.
- **transcribe.cpp** documents both numbers: `p` is "the softmax over the allowed set", and `allowed_mass` is "the share of the softmax over every label that falls in the allowed set… A low value means the unrestricted model puts most of its probability outside the allowed set." It also says: "The model is a closed-set classifier with no 'unknown' or 'no speech' label" ([docs/langid.md#L40-L67](https://github.com/handy-computer/transcribe.cpp/blob/5700f127e38dd53c4735ea23ef743b4b538eacd4/docs/langid.md#L40-L67)). The library sets no policy on it.
- **Handy** passes the person's languages as `allowed` ([detector.rs#L81-L95](https://github.com/cjpais/Handy/blob/7bfb2e2c934b798bcf6e3627653aed2439996f89/src-tauri/src/language_detection/detector.rs#L81-L95)). It always takes the top allowed label. `allowed_mass` only appears in a log line: "allowed mass {:.1}%" ([mod.rs#L208-L241](https://github.com/cjpais/Handy/blob/7bfb2e2c934b798bcf6e3627653aed2439996f89/src-tauri/src/language_detection/mod.rs#L208-L241)).
- **OVOS** filters the full softmax to the valid languages and divides by their sum, with `or 1` guarding a zero sum ([__init__.py#L125-L138](https://github.com/OpenVoiceOS/ovos-audio-transformer-plugin-speechbrain-langdetect/blob/ed103be58c089b87899fc1a3bd7ee855f9fb9605/ovos_audio_transformer_plugin_speechbrain_langdetect/__init__.py#L125-L138)). Its own example is JFK's native English speech, restricted to en/es, at only **0.598** ([#L147-L149](https://github.com/OpenVoiceOS/ovos-audio-transformer-plugin-speechbrain-langdetect/blob/ed103be58c089b87899fc1a3bd7ee855f9fb9605/ovos_audio_transformer_plugin_speechbrain_langdetect/__init__.py#L147-L149)). That run used the default CommonLanguage model ([#L34](https://github.com/OpenVoiceOS/ovos-audio-transformer-plugin-speechbrain-langdetect/blob/ed103be58c089b87899fc1a3bd7ee855f9fb9605/ovos_audio_transformer_plugin_speechbrain_langdetect/__init__.py#L34)), not VoxLingua107.
- **Whisper** already does a masked softmax over its language tokens ([decoding.py#L59-L64](https://github.com/openai/whisper/blob/86098128c0b4f24f0e2aa2994de830614b474227/whisper/decoding.py#L59-L64)). A user asked to restrict it to French and English because "with language=None i got mandarin transcription". The answer was to pick the more probable of the two from `probs` ([openai/whisper#849](https://github.com/openai/whisper/discussions/849)).
- The VoxLingua107 HF discussion "Limiting the number of languages for detection" got a community answer: softmax the classifier output and take the top-k ([discussion #5](https://huggingface.co/speechbrain/lang-id-voxlingua107-ecapa/discussions/5)). No maintainer replied.

### Thresholds on the top language

- **faster-whisper:** `language_detection_threshold=0.5` over all languages, on 30 s segments (`language_detection_segments=1`). It stops at the first segment whose top probability beats 0.5. Otherwise it takes a majority vote of the per-segment winners and reports the best probability seen ([transcribe.py#L1811-L1886](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper/blob/9e472ae9de8bb374552f531f752654554b76b2d6/faster_whisper/transcribe.py#L1811-L1886)). The design comes from [openai/whisper PR #676](https://github.com/openai/whisper/pull/676), still open: "the first 30 second segment… may be silent, or too noisy. In such a scenario, the model may detect an arbitrary language."
- **Kesha:** `LANG_CONFIDENCE_FLOOR = 0.5` keeps a low-confidence audio guess out of the reported `lang` ([language-routing.ts#L5-L6](https://github.com/drakulavich/kesha-voice-kit/blob/a43364e7853a62d8515daa3f6cda15fbfe75de43/src/language-routing.ts#L5-L6), [#L60-L75](https://github.com/drakulavich/kesha-voice-kit/blob/a43364e7853a62d8515daa3f6cda15fbfe75de43/src/language-routing.ts#L60-L75)). The spec's reason: on silence, "audio lang-id returns its prior (`nn` at 0.27)" ([spec.md#L189-L195](https://github.com/drakulavich/kesha-voice-kit/blob/a43364e7853a62d8515daa3f6cda15fbfe75de43/openspec/specs/language-detection/spec.md#L189-L195)). A separate **0.95** threshold lets audio LID override text LID, but only when it names a language outside the ASR set ([#L37-L45](https://github.com/drakulavich/kesha-voice-kit/blob/a43364e7853a62d8515daa3f6cda15fbfe75de43/src/language-routing.ts#L37-L45)).
- **Gladia:** `language_switch_confidence_threshold = 0.95` ([config.py#L39-L40](https://github.com/gladiaio/realtime-multilingual-asr-router/blob/4e25d6489624db46dca627a59c35ad0222c2636b/src/realtime_multilingual_asr_router/config.py#L39-L40)). It is checked only `if session_state.language_confirmed`, so the first decision has no threshold. A low-confidence window is "ignored", not counted against anyone ([language_switch.py#L36-L64](https://github.com/gladiaio/realtime-multilingual-asr-router/blob/4e25d6489624db46dca627a59c35ad0222c2636b/src/realtime_multilingual_asr_router/coordinator/language_switch.py#L36-L64)).
- **NeMo Curator** keeps SpeechBrain's top probability (`exp(score)`) as a confidence ([speechbrain.py#L164-L174](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Curator/blob/8a9a217c90127d424a8f503cb948698d7203002a/nemo_curator/models/audio/lid/speechbrain.py#L164-L174)) but never thresholds it. Selection works by agreement (below).
- **The VoxLingua107 model card** says only that `prediction[1].exp()` is "the linear-scale likelihood" ([README.md#L300-L307](https://huggingface.co/speechbrain/lang-id-voxlingua107-ecapa/blob/0253049ae131d6a4be1c4f0d8b0ff483a0f8c8e9/README.md#L300-L307)). speech-swift's docs: "Product thresholds and unknown-language rejection should be calibrated with representative accents, noise, short clips, and out-of-inventory languages" ([language-identification.md#L50-L60](https://github.com/soniqo/speech-swift/blob/f8ca61de3902b22e3e3a1434c680c1b21d8d48af/docs/models/language-identification.md#L50-L60)).

### Margins and aggregation across windows

- **Handy:** `UNSURE_RATIO = 1.5`, documented as "A pick less than this many times as likely as the runner-up is logged as uncertain (with two languages: p < 0.6). It is used either way" ([mod.rs#L68-L73](https://github.com/cjpais/Handy/blob/7bfb2e2c934b798bcf6e3627653aed2439996f89/src-tauri/src/language_detection/mod.rs#L68-L73)).
- **speech-swift** splits long input into non-overlapping windows and sums `count * exp(logProbability)`, then divides. That is a duration-weighted **mean of probabilities** over all 107 labels ([SpeechLanguageIdentifier.swift#L164-L165](https://github.com/soniqo/speech-swift/blob/f8ca61de3902b22e3e3a1434c680c1b21d8d48af/Sources/SpeechLanguageID/SpeechLanguageIdentifier.swift#L164-L165), [#L206-L245](https://github.com/soniqo/speech-swift/blob/f8ca61de3902b22e3e3a1434c680c1b21d8d48af/Sources/SpeechLanguageID/SpeechLanguageIdentifier.swift#L206-L245)). A mean of probabilities caps any one window's weight at its duration. A sum of log-probabilities lets one window veto, which is why Carbon needs its −10 clip **(inference)**.
- **Majority vote of per-window argmax:** OLMoASR, per video ([tag_audio_lang.py#L219-L253](https://github.com/allenai/OLMoASR/blob/8e8032b6f78f7e3a726851b98e1689e91b5395b9/scripts/data/filtering/tag_audio_lang.py#L219-L253)). NeMo `get_label`, over `num_segments` random segments, logits argmax then `Counter.most_common` ([label_models.py#L637-L681](https://github.com/NVIDIA/NeMo/blob/a837f4e588ca778f46e586a8ad1acf5dc9a244a7/nemo/collections/asr/models/label_models.py#L637-L681)). Also faster-whisper's fallback above, and the accent paper's chunking experiment (below).
- **Agreement between models instead of a threshold:**
  - **NeMo Curator** accepts Whisper's answer outright if it is English. Otherwise it needs SpeechBrain and Whisper to agree (and IndicCanary too for Indic languages), else the clip is "skipped due to disagreement between langID models" ([lid_selection.py#L100-L102, L204-L240](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Curator/blob/8a9a217c90127d424a8f503cb948698d7203002a/nemo_curator/stages/audio/postprocessing/lid_selection.py#L100-L240)).
  - **hayamimi** measured that agreement between whisper-tiny and SenseVoice beats either one alone at every length. On clean audio at 2 s, one model is right 61% of the time; when the two agree they are right 98% of the time. Its policy: switch language only on agreement, until the length at which one model reaches 95% ([docs/eval/lid.md#L104-L116](https://github.com/oboroge0/hayamimi/blob/09c8081420c7c88374eb24ae533ea470de794203/docs/eval/lid.md#L104-L116)).
  - **ESPnet OWSM v4** stores the VoxLingua107 label next to fastText's label of the transcript and of the previous utterance, for filtering ([lid.py#L98-L100, L120-L164](https://github.com/espnet/espnet/blob/0d136c5456d403cb2c0a4c13e76e8136e32e9b7a/egs2/owsm_v4/s2t1/local/lid.py#L98-L164)).
  - **Kesha** prefers text LID and falls back to audio ([language-routing.ts#L55-L75](https://github.com/drakulavich/kesha-voice-kit/blob/a43364e7853a62d8515daa3f6cda15fbfe75de43/src/language-routing.ts#L55-L75)).

The VoxLingua107 training clips were cut to **2–20 s, "the majority… between 4 and 10 seconds"** ([Valk & Alumäe 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12998), §2.1.3). Carbon's 4 s windows sit at the short end of what the model saw in training **(inference)**.

### Rejecting non-speech, music and hold music

- Every live app here gates with **VAD**: Handy ("silence is always cut out by VAD first", [transcription.rs#L1097-L1112](https://github.com/cjpais/Handy/blob/7bfb2e2c934b798bcf6e3627653aed2439996f89/src-tauri/src/managers/transcription.rs#L1097-L1112)), Gladia (Silero, with LID scheduled after VAD pauses, [scheduler.py#L20](https://github.com/gladiaio/realtime-multilingual-asr-router/blob/4e25d6489624db46dca627a59c35ad0222c2636b/src/realtime_multilingual_asr_router/lid/scheduler.py#L20)), faster-whisper's optional `vad_filter`, and CoEDL. None adds a candidate-mass test.
- **Probability floors** on the top language exist for silence that slipped through: Kesha's 0.5 against `nn` at 0.27, Vexa's 0.3, faster-whisper's 0.5 plus majority vote.
- **Whisper's `no_speech_prob`** (threshold 0.6, combined with `logprob_threshold` −1.0) skips silent segments during *transcription* ([transcribe.py#L45-L46, L298-L308](https://github.com/openai/whisper/blob/86098128c0b4f24f0e2aa2994de830614b474227/whisper/transcribe.py#L45-L308)). Its language detection does not use it.
- In VoxLingua107's own validation, **7.5% of the uncleaned segments "contained non-speech"** ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12998), §2.3). So the model was trained with some music and noise labelled as languages. That would explain confident labels on non-speech **(inference)**.
- I found no project or issue that measured VoxLingua107 on hold music specifically.

### Accented and L2 speech

- **Model card:** "Based on subjective experiments, it doesn't work well on speech with a foreign accent" ([README.md#L321-L328](https://huggingface.co/speechbrain/lang-id-voxlingua107-ecapa/blob/0253049ae131d6a4be1c4f0d8b0ff483a0f8c8e9/README.md#L321-L328)).
- **[speechbrain#2826](https://github.com/speechbrain/speechbrain/issues/2826)** (open since 2025-02-14): Indian-accented English is "predict[ed as] some other languages but not English". The reporter's run gives `['tl: Tagalog']` at 0.31. A maintainer asked for audio, and no fix followed. This is the same mass on `tl` that Carbon measured on French-accented English.
- **[openai/whisper#475](https://github.com/openai/whisper/discussions/475):** a Finnish speaker reports that Whisper "picks up other languages that I speak as Finnish… presumably because of my thick Finnish accent". An OpenAI maintainer replied: "the language detection might be focusing too much on the phonetics and less on the linguistic content, making it less robust to accents."
- **VoxLingua107 paper, Table 4:** among the "most common errors" on the dev set is **English → Welsh**. The authors put this down to the bad quality of the Welsh data, not to accents ([arXiv 2011.12998](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12998), §3.1). Note that VoxLingua107 English is YouTube English with all its accents. The Welsh, Breton and Tagalog classes absorb some of that variety **(inference)**.
- **Bafna & Wiesner, "LID Models are Actually Accent Classifiers" (Interspeech 2025, [arXiv 2506.00628](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00628), [code](https://github.com/niyatibafna/mitigating-accent-bias-in-lid/blob/229cb1ceac1a8421e8328c4421df6ba7f33b24df/README.md#L1-L5))**, which evaluates this exact checkpoint:
  - ECAPA-TDNN is right on 87.6% of mainstream-accent English in CommonVoice and 73% in EdAcc, but only about **56–57% on L2-accented English** (§5).
  - Table 2 gives a FLEURS mean of 89.3 against EdAcc 47.9 / CommonVoice 34.5 (mean) or 55.8 / 57.0 (macro). The paper's text and table disagree on which macro number belongs to which set **(unverified which is right)**.
  - **L2 French: 80.1%, against 97.5% for mainstream French accents.**
  - Errors are mostly "the speaker's native language or a related language": Dutch-accented English → Dutch is 82.6% of the errors (§5.1).
  - **Longer context helps ECAPA.** In a majority vote over T-second chunks of > 10 s recordings, non-US English accuracy on EdAcc rose from 23.5% at 0.5 s chunks to **65.1% at 8 s** (Table 4, §6.2).
  - The proposed fix trains a new classifier on ECAPA's last-layer embeddings plus phoneme sequences from a wav2vec2 phoneme recogniser (`ET+phoneseqs-train`). It is much better on accented speech, at the cost of a second, large model.
  - The paper gives **no per-L1 result for French speakers of English**.
- **Fix used in practice: a small classifier on ECAPA embeddings.** The model card lists this as the second intended use: "an utterance-level feature (embedding) extractor, for creating a dedicated language ID model on your own data" ([README.md#L254-L259](https://huggingface.co/speechbrain/lang-id-voxlingua107-ecapa/blob/0253049ae131d6a4be1c4f0d8b0ff483a0f8c8e9/README.md#L254-L259)).
  - **CoEDL** trains a balanced logistic regression on the 256-d `encode_batch` embeddings of a few labelled clips per language, for English vs Muruwari. The toy example is the author saying words in French and English ([sli.py#L43-L78](https://github.com/CoEDL/vad-sli-asr/blob/5f61cab21ce1d6006d39ecb6b62d245bb39a9a8c/scripts/helpers/sli.py#L43-L78), [README.md#L49-L99](https://github.com/CoEDL/vad-sli-asr/blob/5f61cab21ce1d6006d39ecb6b62d245bb39a9a8c/README.md#L49-L99)).
  - **hayamimi** did the same with 40 FLEURS clips per language. At 4 s on clean audio it scored 92% overall against 78% for the 107-way argmax, but 70% on Japanese ([lid_candidates.md#L107-L190](https://github.com/oboroge0/hayamimi/blob/09c8081420c7c88374eb24ae533ea470de794203/docs/eval/lid_candidates.md#L107-L190)). That is native speech, not accents.
  - The VoxLingua107 paper found the same on KALAKA-3: a logistic regression trained on about 3 h of in-domain data beat one trained on VoxLingua107 itself (§3.2, Table 5).

## What this means for Carbon

Carbon's pipeline already matches or exceeds what the others do on most axes: VAD gating, candidate restriction, duration weighting, a minimum amount of speech, a margin, and a give-up. The outlier is the **candidate-mass floor** (`minimumCandidateProbability = 0.1`). No other project gates on it. Carbon's own numbers show it cannot separate the two cases it sits between:

- Accented English measured **0.005–0.24** for en+fr.
- The hold-music fixture is **0.082**.
- The ADR already records that the floor drops about 36% of French-accented TTS English.

On the maintainer's real voice it drops most windows. Yet the renormalized en/fr split in those windows is right (0.8–1.0 English in 8 of 9). So the information is there, and the floor throws it away.

Recommendations, in order (all **(inference)** unless cited):

1. **Stop using the candidate mass as a speech test.** "How much of the 107-way mass fell on en+fr" measures how native-sounding the accent is, not whether the window is speech. That matches the paper's "LID models are accent classifiers" and speechbrain#2826's `tl` result. Rejecting non-speech is VAD's job, as in every other project here. Carbon's own measurement agrees: about 80 min of instrumental music gave **zero windows past Silero VAD**.
2. **Before changing the threshold, check whether hold music passes the VAD at all.** Run Silero at 0.85 on the hold-music recording behind the 0.082 fixture. If VAD drops it, the floor has nothing left to do for music; remove it or lower it to a sanity value (e.g. 0.001). If hold music with vocals does pass VAD, do not keep the floor at 0.1. Use one of points 3–4.
3. **Apply any such rule per stream.** Hold music, ringtones and the other side's audio arrive on **Them** (system audio). The accented voice that is being dropped is the person's own, on **Me** (microphone). Skip the floor on Me, or make it far lower there. That keeps the hold-music test meaningful and stops penalising the user's own accent. Carbon already feeds both streams separately (`LanguageIdentification.hear(_:on:)`).
4. **If a non-candidate rule is still needed, test "concentrated elsewhere", not "little on the candidates".** Speech in an unchecked language gives one confident non-candidate label (the test's German window has de 0.95; the hold-music fixture has oc 0.9). The accented windows scatter over fa/tl/br/lv. A rule like "drop the window if one non-candidate label has ≥ 0.5–0.9" mirrors Kesha's 0.95 "confident other language" test, and would keep scattered accented windows. This needs the logged per-label distributions of the 9 accented windows and of the real hold-music recording to confirm. **(unverified)**
5. **Re-run the vote offline on the logged windows without the floor**, before tuning anything else. With 8 of 9 windows at an English share of 0.8–1.0, the mean log-odds may or may not reach ln 20 ≈ 3.0. A 0.8 share is only ln 4 ≈ 1.4, and the −10 clip lets near-certain windows dominate. That tells whether `minimumMargin` also needs to move. Gladia's 0.95 (about 19:1) is the only comparable published margin, and it is applied per window, not as a mean. Handy calls anything above 1.5:1 good enough to use.
6. **Try longer windows on accented speech.** In Bafna & Wiesner Table 4, ECAPA's accuracy on accented English rises steadily from 0.5 s to 8 s chunks. The training data is mostly 4–10 s. Scoring 8 s windows instead of 4 s, with the same 8 s minimum, costs nothing extra. It may lift both the en+fr mass and the en share for the maintainer's voice. Measure it on the recorded windows.
7. **Medium term: a per-user head on the embeddings.** This is the documented fix for accents and domain shift (model card's second use; CoEDL; hayamimi (d); the VoxLingua107 paper §3.2). A logistic regression or nearest-centroid classifier over the 256-d ECAPA embedding for {en, fr} could learn from the person's own Meetings once their language is settled. The Me stream is one speaker, which is exactly the case a small in-domain head handles best. This needs the Core ML export to expose the embedding (speech-swift's pipeline has the 256-d embedding before the classifier: [language-identification.md#L10-L17](https://github.com/soniqo/speech-swift/blob/f8ca61de3902b22e3e3a1434c680c1b21d8d48af/docs/models/language-identification.md#L10-L17); whether the Carbon export exposes it is **(unverified)**).
8. **Keep the lexical tie-breaker in reserve.** Curator, hayamimi, ESPnet and Kesha all pair the acoustic model with a second signal and accept only agreement. The ADR's SpeechTranscriber race is Carbon's equivalent, and it is the right veto when the acoustic margin is small on accented speech.
9. **Keep `gaveUp` falling back to the default language**, never to English. That is unchanged from the earlier notes, and Handy does the same: it uses "the first preferred language" whenever it cannot detect ([mod.rs#L173-L195](https://github.com/cjpais/Handy/blob/7bfb2e2c934b798bcf6e3627653aed2439996f89/src-tauri/src/language_detection/mod.rs#L173-L195)).
