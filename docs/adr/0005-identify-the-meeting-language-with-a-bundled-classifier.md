# Identify the Meeting Language with a bundled acoustic classifier, once per Meeting

Apple has no public spoken language identification API (docs/research/language-detection.md), so Carbon ships SpeechBrain's VoxLingua107 ECAPA-TDNN classifier (Core ML, 43 MB, Apache-2.0) in the app. When the person checked several Meeting Languages, it scores the first seconds of both streams' speech, restricted to those languages, and decides once: a different winner re-transcribes the whole Meeting through the existing language switch. This beats racing one SpeechTranscriber per candidate language (twice the transcription during the window, more per extra language) and Whisper's language detection (larger, slower, worse on 2 s clips); see docs/research/offline-spoken-language-identification.md.

## Consequences

- The app grows by 44 MB (43 MB classifier, 1 MB speech gate), and the model is loaded only for a Meeting that needs it and let go once its language is decided.
- One decision per Meeting: a real change of language mid-Meeting is not followed, because the switch re-transcribes the whole Meeting in one language.
- The classifier judges accent, not words: English spoken with a strong French accent can be identified as French. The thresholds stay conservative until they are calibrated on recorded Meetings, and the native SpeechTranscriber race remains the candidate tie-breaker.
- The classifier is closed-set, and restricted to English and French it calls music, ringtones and noise English more often than French. So only speech reaches it: a speech gate, Silero VAD v6.2.1 (Core ML, about 1 MB, MIT, shipped in the app), judges each stream's audio in 256 ms chunks with its own state per stream, and only the chunks it gives at least 0.85 (FluidAudio's default) are scored. On synthetic clips it passed 95–99% of English and French speech chunks, with or without music under them, and 0–3% of music, noise and notification sounds; the loudness gate it replaces let all of those through. Apple's SpeechDetector was not an option: it gave no results on macOS 27 and is documented only as gating a transcriber.
- The gate calls Silero through plain Core ML, behind CarbonCore's `SpeechGate` port, rather than FluidAudio's `VadManager`. The app doesn't link FluidAudio: only the diarization helper does, in its own process. `VadManager` would bring the whole package (and its model downloader) into the app for one model with three inputs and three outputs, and its streaming state API would tie the port to FluidAudio's types. The cost is following the model's I/O by hand if the model changes.
- The vote still drops any window the checked languages together score under 10% over all 107 labels. The gate cannot do that part: it keeps speech in a language nobody checked, and the occasional non-speech chunk it lets through. Measured on TTS speech and non-speech recordings, that rule drops 98% of non-speech and keeps 97% of English and French speech, but also about 36% of French-accented English.
- The speech gate's model is used for the same Meetings as the classifier: it is loaded on the Meeting's first audio, before the classifier's first window of speech, and let go with the classifier. A Meeting started right after another has its audio judged only once the other's identification has let both go.
- A language the person did not check is never identified, and a Meeting whose language stays unclear keeps the language it started in. The vote gives up after 40 windows, counted or dropped, so a Meeting in an unchecked language is not scored for its whole length.
- The person's choice from the menu or the notification ends identification for that Meeting.
